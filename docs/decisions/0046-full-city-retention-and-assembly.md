# Decision 0046: full-city retention and assembly partitioning

Date: 2026-08-15

Status: **accepted for the measurements and the assembly-partitioning decision
(D1, D3, D5)**. D2 (retain) records a decision that is already in force for the
shipped subset and extends it to a full-city payload. D4 (retire superseded
payload directories) is **RECOMMENDED AND NOT EXECUTED**: deleting retained
artifacts needs explicit operator authorization, which this task did not have and
did not seek.

No release was assembled, no artifact was published, no wave was materialized, no
payload directory was written or deleted, and no runtime budget was changed by
this task. Every GLB regenerated below was hashed, counted and dropped.

## Context

Task T001 asks two questions that ADR 0040 deliberately left open: whether the
generated city can be regenerated byte-for-byte from what is committed, and what
a full-city retention would cost against the contracts that already exist.

The first question had to be settled before the second could mean anything. A
storage decision about bytes nobody can reproduce is a decision about bytes
nobody can check.

**Plan hashes are not comparable across waves.** A V3 plan hash covers the wave's
seed, generated instant and tool identity as well as the sourced polygon, and the
six wave seeds are committed constants that differ. No comparison in this task
crosses a wave boundary. `--profile <waveId>` exists on the `plans` stage for

`distributions.json` because a different profile *may* produce a different
artifact.

Run, it does not. `plans --profile lower-manhattan --force` produced an
off-record artifact whose SHA-256 is
`403125b45eb5b66a53cf33d912a6360efbb5b62ff30e8ce891431ef3ff8f0131` — **identical

the plan-stage distributions carry ring-vertex counts, tier counts and refusal

depend on the wave's seed.

Where the per-wave profiles *are* load-bearing is Proof 2. A wave's profile
supplies the `releaseId` that namespaces every inventory and evidence-shard id
and the uncertainty string that every asset carries, all of which land in GLB
metadata. Running one wave's profile over another wave's cells would move
`generatedAssetBytes`. The exact byte-total agreement in the table above is only
obtainable with each wave's own profile, and that is what makes it evidence.

### Correction to ADR 0032 — the 76% does not hold island-wide

ADR 0032 measured the UV term at **76% of the texture delta** on Block 835's
fourteen buildings and called it "the single most important number in this ADR
for planning purposes". **Island-wide the split inverts**: the UV term is
33.2% – **38.3%** – 58.4% of the texture delta.

It could not have held. The image term is a fixed two or three tiles per
building regardless of size; the UV term follows a vertex count whose island
median is far below Block 835's. The direction of the atlas argument is unchanged
and in fact strengthened — an atlas removes *more* of the citywide texture cost
than the fourteen-building table implies — but the magnitude claim in ADR 0032 is
a statement about fourteen large buildings and must not be quoted as a citywide
one.

What survives an atlas is the UV term. `TEXCOORD_0` is per-vertex geometry data;
an atlas changes which region of a texture a vertex addresses, not whether it
addresses one.

### Instrument validation

The same instrument was run against Block 835's committed `-v3t` census. Embedded
image bytes and texture counts agree for all fourteen buildings. The
`uvAndJsonByteDelta` reads 248–268 B **low** — a spread of 20 B across vertex
counts from 1,318 to 206,124, a factor of 156. That is fixed package metadata
this pass does not reconstruct, not a per-vertex scaling error, so the island
slope is unaffected. It is reported rather than subtracted.

### Two island totals

- **44,295** — what the six waves actually materialized.
- **45,116** — what a full-city retention would carry if the 821 *recoverable*
  refusals were recovered (`source-height-below-grammar-minimum` 384,
  `ring-vertex-count-unsupported` 324, `ring-area-below-floor` 113). The 43
  irrecoverable refusals (`ring-neck-below-grammar-minimum` 39, `ring-not-simple`
  4) and the 35 asset-stage `volume-identity-failed` are not recoverable by

**The 821 are vertex-count-heavy.** 324 of them are rings *above* the grammar's
64-vertex cap, so recovering them adds buildings into the `ring-41-64` stratum —

recovered full city grows faster than its building count.

wave boundary is already the unit that rights, approval and promotion are
recorded against.

## D2 — Retain the generated bytes. Do not plan on browser-side regeneration.

Regeneration is **byte-exact** (Proof 3: every shipped GLB reproduced from the
pinned snapshot and this repository's code) and **cheap** (5.3–8.9 minutes for
the whole island, single-threaded). Both facts argue for regenerating rather than
storing.

They do not settle it, because **the browser cannot regenerate.** The generator
is a Node-side pipeline over a 291 MB pinned snapshot with a shard-checksum gate
in front of it; a session that had to run it would have to ship the snapshot, the
grammar and the writer to the client and then spend minutes before drawing a
building. Regeneration is an *operator* capability, not a runtime one.

The strongest argument for retention is not convenience, it is **provability**.
`validateProceduralTextureGlb` (`src/release/multi-lod-assembly.ts:785-849`)
regenerates every procedural tile and requires each embedded image to be
**byte-identical** to what this repository's own rasterizer produces; two images
resolving to one class is also refused. A retained payload is therefore not an
opaque blob that has to be trusted — it is a blob that can be *re-derived and
checked*, and the committed per-file inventories pin every byte of it whether or
not the payload directory exists.

**Decision: retain the generated payload bytes; keep the payload directories
untracked; keep the per-file inventories committed.**

### Reversal procedure, including the direction that is hard

Reversing retention is:

1. delete the payload directory (untracked; costs nothing but disk);
2. revert the wave's `payload-inventory.json` in git.

Step 1 is free and instantaneous. **Step 2 is the hard direction and is stated
plainly: a committed inventory is in git history forever.** Reverting it removes
it from the working tree, not from the repository's size. A retention decision
that commits inventories is therefore *not* symmetrically reversible, and the
weight below is the price of that asymmetry.

## D3 — Keep full `files[]` inventories, per wave. Do not go digest-only.

Committed inventory weight today: **1,184,375 B across 10 files and 4,518 file
rows — 262 B per row.** A single full-city inventory at 91,115 rows projects to
**22.8 MiB in git**, or roughly 4–6 MiB per wave.

The alternative considered was a **digest-only** inventory: one root checksum per
wave instead of a row per file. It would cost a few hundred bytes per wave.

**Decision: keep the full `files[]` form, split per wave.** A digest-only
inventory can say *that* a payload drifted; it cannot say *which file* drifted,
and the fail-closed refusal in D5 would degrade from naming an artifact to naming
a directory. The per-file rows are exactly what makes D2's provability argument
true. 22.8 MiB of committed text against a 4.5 GiB payload is a 0.5% index
overhead, and it is the part that survives the payload's deletion.

Recorded consequence: the projection is **22.8 MiB**, not the ~70 MB the task
plan carried. The task plan's figure was not reproduced by measurement and is
superseded by the table above.

## D6 — `lod_1` asymmetries, recorded rather than assumed

Three facts about `lod_1` that a full-city retention has to carry, none of which
is a decision this task may make:

1. **`lod_1` is untextured by contract, not by omission.** Detail tiles ride on
   `lod_0` alone in every wave profile, carried unchanged from the Block 835
   `-v3t` decision: `lod_1` is selected beyond 250 m, where a 128-pixel joint is
   far below a screen pixel. The writer enforces it
   (`midtown-core-v3-materialization.ts`: `const texture = lodIndex === 0 ?
   profile.texture : null`). There is therefore no UV term and no image term for
   `lod_1` anywhere in the decomposition above.
2. **`lod_1` is not shipped today.** `MIDTOWN_CORE_SHIPPED_LOD_ID` is `lod_0`,
   and all five generated waves ship `lod_0` only — 470 of the 498 shipped
   assets. Block 835 is the sole exception: it ships **both** LODs, 28 assets for
   14 buildings, because it travels the reference-package promotion path rather
   than the wave materializer.
3. **City-wide `lod_1` serving is a NEW decision, deferred to T005.** Its
   consequence is named here so it is not discovered later: the runtime cache is
   keyed **per artifact**, so a resident `lod_1` occupies an entry exactly as a
   `lod_0` does. Serving both LODs island-wide doubles the entry demand against
   `EXTERIOR_RUNTIME_BUDGETS.maxCacheEntries` before a single byte of the 1.490
   GB `lod_1` payload is counted. That is a cache-design question of the same
   kind ADR 0040 raised for the shared shard cache, and it must be decided with
   it rather than after it.

## Corrections to committed records

**`wave-bytes.json` labels wave w00 `textured: true` and its per-wave note claims
"Wave w00's shipped LOD 0 carries procedural detail tiles". This is wrong.** The
shipped Block 835 release `manhattan-exterior-cells-20260811-v3` is
texture-free: its GLBs declare `images: 0`, `textures: 0` and carry no
`TEXCOORD_0`, and its own approval scope text says "deterministically generated,
**TEXTURE-FREE** exterior geometry". The textured `-v3t` package exists as a
census record only and was never published to `public/data`.

The error is a label, not an arithmetic one: wave w00 is excluded from every
island mean in that record for an independent reason (14 reference-authored
buildings), so no committed figure moves. **The record is left byte-identical and
the correction is recorded here**, because adjusting a gated census record to
match a later reading is exactly what this task's own instrument refuses to do.

## What this task did NOT establish

- **No rendered frame.** Every figure here is arithmetic and hashing. Nothing was
  looked at, and nothing here is visual acceptance.
- **No decision on whether a full city SHOULD be retained.** D1–D3 say what it
  costs and how it must be partitioned if it is. Whether to build it remains
  bound by ADR 0040 D1's approval gate and the goal's acceptance criteria as
  approved.
- **No measurement of decoded GPU bytes, frame time or cache-thrash cost.** The
  `lod_1` cache-occupancy consequence in D6 is a structural statement about entry
  counts, not a measurement of what happens when the cache is exceeded.
- **No rights re-evaluation.** Nothing here widens any approval envelope, and the
  per-cell rights gate ADR 0040 named for mass shipping is untouched.
